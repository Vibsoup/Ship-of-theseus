STUDY 002 · TIME & CHANGE

Does a thing remain itself through time?

We recognise things partly through their histories. A repaired ship, a childhood home, a well-used book: their materials change, but the story connecting yesterday to today may remain.

01

Gradual change

One plank is replaced. Then another. The ship remains in use throughout the process.

Does an unbroken history preserve identity?

02

Interrupted existence

The ship is dismantled, left apart, and assembled again later using replacement parts.

Does a break in its existence change what it is?

03

Lost history

Imagine that every record of the ship disappears, even though the vessel itself continues to sail.

Can something remain the same if its past is forgotten?

A QUESTION WITHOUT AN ANSWER KEY

Continuity is one possible basis for identity, not a guaranteed solution. Two people can know the same history and still disagree about whether the object is the same one.

STUDY 003 · MEMORY & SELF

If your memories changed, would you still be you?

We often tell the story of who we are through what we remember. But memory is imperfect, identity changes, and forgetting does not necessarily make someone a different person.

A QUESTION OF THE SELF

Imagine waking up with a different past.

You have the same body, the same name, and the same place in the world. But some of your personal memories are missing. Other people remember experiences you cannot recall.

Which parts of you would remain, even if your memories changed?

01

Memory

Your memories connect your present self to your past experiences.

If you forgot an important event, would it change who you are?

02

Personality

Your habits, values, and reactions shape how you experience the world.

If your personality changed over time, would you still be you?

03

Relationships

Other people may remember parts of your life that you have forgotten.

Can relationships preserve a sense of self beyond memory?

LEAVE A NOTE FOR THE ARCHIVE

Memory is one theory of personal identity, not the only one. Bodily continuity, psychological traits, relationships, and lived experience may matter in different ways.

FROM THE RESEARCH DESK

Identity is not a percentage.

We are examining whether material, memory, appearance, function, and continuity shape how people judge sameness. The archive collects judgments; it does not decide what identity truly is.
